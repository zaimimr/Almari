# Almari rule candidates: modest Western and hijab styling

**Before you encode these, two things to know.** Haute Hijab's blog returns HTTP 402 to automated fetches, so anything credited to Haute Hijab comes from search-result excerpts of their pages, not from reading the full articles. Most of the other sources are brand blogs (Aab, Abaya Buth, Riwaya, Worood, Dainty Jewells), so the evidence is practitioner opinion and not research. The strength labels show how often the same advice turned up across sources, not that any of it is proven. I found no usable Vogue Arabia, Modanisa or Inayah styling article, so none of the rules below rely on them.

**Design principle:** treat every coverage-related choice as a per-user preference (sleeve length, neckline, trousers vs skirt only, belts allowed, fitted vs loose). The engine should never judge whether an outfit is "modest enough". It should only apply the standard the user chose.

## 1. Hijab pairing

1. IF the outfit has a printed top, dress or skirt THEN prefer a solid hijab (strength: strong) - Haute Hijab (matching guide, excerpt); Arabia Weddings; Haute Hijab (print-on-print, excerpt).
2. IF the hijab is solid and the outfit is printed THEN prefer a hijab colour taken from the print, ideally a secondary colour of the print rather than the background (strength: strong for "taken from the print", weak for "secondary colour", which is opinion) - Haute Hijab (excerpts).
3. IF the outfit is entirely solid or neutral THEN allow a printed or bold-coloured hijab as the focal point (strength: medium) - Haute Hijab (excerpt); Worood ("use scarves and hijabs to introduce color, texture, or pattern").
4. IF the hijab is printed AND the outfit has a print THEN allow it only when (a) the prints share a colour family, (b) the prints are of a similar type or scale, and (c) at least one solid garment separates them (strength: medium) - Haute Hijab "How to rock print hijabs with print clothing" (excerpt: "wear something solid to break up the prints… keep the colors in the same family").
5. IF the hijab print and the outfit print are different types (for example animal print with floral, or stripes with floral) THEN avoid (strength: medium) - Haute Hijab (excerpt).
6. IF the outfit has two or more saturated colours THEN prefer a neutral hijab (beige, grey, black, ivory, camel) (strength: strong) - Haute Hijab; Muslim Girl.
7. IF the user selects "monochrome" or "tonal" THEN prefer a hijab in the same hue family as the main garment, but a different lightness or texture (strength: medium) - Haute Hijab (excerpt: "go monotone and match… or wear a contrasting hijab"); Riwaya (light abaya with dark hijab, or the reverse).
8. IF none of the rules above applies THEN prefer a hijab that matches either the bottom garment, the bag or the shoes, since a repeated colour pulls the look together (strength: medium) - Darsonbol; Muslim Girl (bag matched to hijab).
9. IF the hijab is a statement piece (bold print, embellished or velvet) THEN avoid statement jewellery and prefer small, minimal accessories (strength: medium) - Muslim Girl.
10. IF a sheer or chiffon hijab is chosen THEN pair it with an underscarf in a matching or neutral colour (ivory, stone, black or nude) (strength: medium) - Haute Hijab underscarf product range; Aab (neutral slips under sheer layers).

**Fabric vs occasion** (all medium strength unless noted):

11. Jersey: casual, sport or travel. Allow for work only if it has a sheen. Low effort, needs no pins - Haute Hijab fabric guide (excerpt).
12. Modal and viscose: everyday, office and casual. The default "all-rounder" fabric. Rated medium-high for hot weather - Haute Hijab; Aab; Hanm.
13. Chiffon: dressy (dinner, wedding, Eid) but also fine everyday. Slippery, so it needs an underscarf - Haute Hijab; Aab ("pair with heels and chiffon hijabs" for evening).
14. Silk and satin: occasion wear, or an elevated everyday piece - Haute Hijab.
15. Cotton and linen: hot weather and casual - Riwaya; Hanm.
16. Wool, cashmere blends and velvet: cold weather. Velvet also counts as festive - Riwaya winter guide; East Essence.

## 2. Silhouette and proportion

17. IF the bottom is voluminous (wide-leg trousers, maxi or pleated skirt) THEN prefer a neater or more structured top, or tuck it in. IF the top is voluminous THEN prefer a straighter bottom. Avoid volume on both halves unless the user's style is "relaxed" (strength: strong) - Abaya Buth; standard stylist balance rule.
18. IF the outfit is a tunic or kurta-length top with trousers THEN prefer straight, slim or wide-leg trousers that end at or below the ankle (strength: medium). Tunic length is personal: some users want it at the knee or longer. Make it a user setting, never a rule - Zalora PH, Seekers Guidance (a scholarly Q&A that gives knee length as one view; treat it as an option, not a default).
19. IF the outfit is a longline or maxi layer (maxi abaya or maxi dress) THEN layers of different lengths add shape. Prefer a cropped jacket or a shorter open layer over it, and avoid three or more layers of the same length (strength: medium) - Abaya Buth layering guide ("uniform length creates visual flatness").
20. IF the user wants to look taller or more streamlined THEN prefer a column of colour (top and bottom in the same or similar colour, shoes matching the bottom, and a hijab that continues the column) plus an open contrasting layer (strength: medium) - Inside Out Style.
21. IF a cardigan is layered over a dress or tunic THEN prefer fine knits over bulky ones (strength: medium) - Inside Out Style.
22. **Belt over an abaya, dress or long cardigan: contested.** Stylists recommend it to add shape and a modern look (Abaya Buth, Riwaya). Many wearers avoid it because it shows the body's shape (Seekers Guidance; r/Hijabis). Rule: IF the user setting `beltsOverOuterwear` is true THEN suggest a belt for long, shapeless pieces. Otherwise never suggest one (strength: weak, preference-gated).
23. IF the outfit is a dress with a fitted waist THEN prefer A-line or fit-and-flare shapes for formal events (strength: weak) - Dainty Jewells.

## 3. Layering for coverage

These rules fire only when the user's coverage preference calls for them.

24. IF the top or dress is short-sleeved or sleeveless AND the user prefers long sleeves THEN add a long-sleeve base (a fitted long-sleeve tee or turtleneck) under it, or an open layer (cardigan, blazer, kimono) over it (strength: strong) - Dainty Jewells; Arabia Weddings; Inside Out Style.
25. IF the dress or skirt is shorter than the user's preferred length THEN layer trousers, wide-leg trousers or jeans underneath, or a longer slip skirt (strength: strong) - Arabia Weddings; Dainty Jewells.
26. IF the dress has a deep or wide neckline THEN layer a turtleneck or high-neck base. The hijab drape also covers the neck (strength: medium) - Dainty Jewells.
27. IF a sheer abaya, kimono or dress is chosen THEN add a full-length slip in a neutral colour (black, white, navy or nude) (strength: strong) - Aab "Back to basics".
28. IF a shirt dress or open abaya is chosen THEN allow it worn open as a coat over a slip, a base layer and trousers (strength: medium) - Aab.
29. IF the outfit is a casual dress or jeans and the user wants coverage THEN suggest a kimono or open abaya as the outer layer (strength: medium) - Aab; Abaya Buth.

## 4. Occasion formality

Give each item a formality score. Raising the score: silk, satin, chiffon, lace and embroidery; heels and pointed flats; clutches; structured blazers; tailored trousers. Lowering it: jersey, denim, sneakers, crossbody bags, totes, hoodies and heavy prints.

30. Work or office: prefer tailored trousers, wide-leg trousers or a midi or maxi skirt; a blouse, shirt or longline tunic; a blazer or long coat; loafers, flats or closed-toe heels; a structured handbag or tote; a neutral solid hijab in modal or matte jersey. Avoid an embellished hijab, large brooches and sequins (strength: strong) - Muslim Girl; Riwaya; Abaya Buth.
31. Everyday casual: prefer jeans, wide-leg trousers or a maxi skirt; a t-shirt, sweater or tunic; a cardigan, denim jacket or kimono; sneakers, flats or sandals; a crossbody or tote; a jersey or modal hijab, where colour and print are welcome (strength: strong) - Dainty Jewells ("dress + denim jacket + sneakers"); Riwaya.
32. Dinner or evening: swap flats for heels and the handbag for a clutch, and move the hijab to chiffon or silk (strength: medium) - Abaya Buth (day-to-night); Aab.
33. Wedding guest or celebration: prefer a maxi dress, a lace or embroidered abaya, or a skirt with a long-sleeve blouse; heels; a clutch; a chiffon or silk hijab; statement or fine jewellery. Avoid jersey, sneakers and totes (strength: strong) - Riwaya; Dainty Jewells; Arabia Weddings.
34. Eid: like a celebration, but prefer richer or brighter colours and embroidery, gold or silver jewellery, and a hijab matched to the outfit (strength: medium) - Riwaya; Abaya Buth. **Regional note:** in the Gulf, Eid is often a new abaya or kaftan, while UK and European wearers often choose dresses or co-ords. Let the user pick her own style; do not assume one.
35. IF the formality gap between any two items is more than one level THEN avoid, except for the deliberate pairing of a casual dress with sneakers, which you can allow (strength: medium) - general stylist practice (opinion).

## 5. Shoes and bags

36. IF formal THEN heels, pointed flats or embellished flats, plus a clutch. IF smart THEN loafers, pointed flats, block heels or ankle boots, plus a structured handbag. IF casual THEN sneakers, flat sandals or boots, plus a crossbody or tote (strength: strong) - Riwaya; Abaya Buth; Dainty Jewells.
37. IF a maxi or floor-length garment is worn THEN prefer shoes "proportional to the length": a slight heel, a platform or a sleek flat. Avoid chunky shoes with a delicate chiffon maxi (strength: weak) - Abaya Buth.
38. IF the bag colour equals the hijab colour or the shoe colour THEN award a bonus. Matching bag and hijab is called out as polished (strength: medium) - Muslim Girl; Darsonbol.
39. IF the shoes are coloured or printed THEN prefer the other accessories to be neutral (strength: weak, opinion).
40. IF the bottom is a trouser or maxi skirt AND the user wants a lengthening effect THEN prefer shoes in the same tone as the bottom (strength: medium) - Inside Out Style.

## 6. Outfit formulas (role combos)

41. Office classic: wide-leg trousers + tucked blouse or shirt + blazer + loafers or block heels + neutral modal hijab + structured handbag.
42. Office tunic: longline tunic + straight trousers + structured blazer + pointed flats + solid hijab - Riwaya, Muslim Girl.
43. Weekend: maxi skirt + tucked knit or t-shirt + long fine cardigan + sneakers or loafers + neutral jersey hijab + crossbody.
44. Layered dress: slip or sleeveless dress + long-sleeve turtleneck base + denim jacket + sneakers + printed hijab picking up a dress colour - Dainty Jewells.
45. Abaya day: open abaya + slip or long-sleeve base + straight trousers + white sneakers + matching or tonal hijab + tote.
46. Abaya evening: closed embellished abaya + heels + chiffon hijab + clutch + fine jewellery - Aab, Riwaya.
47. Shirt-dress coat: shirt dress worn open + slip or jeans + loafers + modal hijab - Aab.
48. Winter: turtleneck + midi or maxi skirt or wide-leg trousers + long wool coat + ankle boots + wool or cashmere-blend hijab tucked into the coat - East Essence, Riwaya.
49. Wedding guest: long-sleeve maxi dress (A-line, lace or embroidered) + heels + chiffon or silk hijab in a print colour + clutch - Dainty Jewells.

## 7. Clashes and mistakes

50. Avoid a printed hijab with a busy printed outfit unless rule 4 passes (strong).
51. Avoid a sparkly or ornate hijab with a work outfit (medium) - Muslim Girl.
52. Avoid a statement hijab plus a statement necklace plus a detailed abaya together. A detailed abaya should have few other accessories (medium) - Abaya Buth; Muslim Girl.
53. Avoid all layers being the same length (medium) - Abaya Buth.
54. Avoid voluminous pieces on both top and bottom with a bulky cardigan (medium).
55. Avoid more than about three unrelated colours plus a print. Coordinated colours across layers "create depth without overwhelming" (medium) - Worood.
56. Avoid a sheer outer layer without a slip, if the user wants opaque coverage (strong) - Aab.
57. Avoid a bulky hijab drape with a tailored coat. Prefer tucked ends (weak) - East Essence.

## 8. Season and weather

58. IF hot THEN prefer cotton, linen, modal or viscose hijabs, loose linen or cotton garments, light colours, sandals or breathable flats, and few layers. Use a lightweight open layer (kimono or shirt dress) for coverage instead of a knit (strong) - Riwaya; Hanm; Aab; Worood.
59. IF cold THEN prefer wool, knit or velvet, a turtleneck base, a long coat, ankle or knee boots, and darker or muted colours (navy, burgundy, khaki, grey). Wool and cashmere-blend hijabs, or a scarf layered over the hijab (strong) - Riwaya; Aab AW24; East Essence.
60. IF mild or between seasons THEN reuse summer pieces with a knit or slip underneath. Aab and Abaya Buth explicitly say to "resist thinking seasonally" (medium).
61. IF rain or wet weather THEN avoid suede, silk and pale chiffon. Prefer jersey or modal, a trench coat, and boots or sneakers (weak, practical inference, no source found).

## Contested, opinion-only and regional notes

- **Belts over abayas** (rule 22): actively debated among wearers and scholars. Make it opt-in.
- **Tunic or top length and trousers vs skirts:** the knee-length "standard" comes from one religious Q&A (Seekers Guidance), not from stylists. Make it a user setting.
- **Fitted vs loose:** the UK, Turkey and Scandinavia show more tailored and layered Western looks. Gulf styling centres on the abaya, often worn open over an outfit. Malaysian and Indonesian styling favours lighter, brighter and floral hijabs with more volume. Turkish styling uses neatly pinned square scarves. Do not hard-code any of these. Offer "style region" or "style preference" as a soft bias (Wikipedia "Types of hijab").
- **Skin-tone colour matching** (warm vs cool) appears in several blogs, but it is colour-theory opinion and belongs to the colour-theory workstream.
- **Prints with prints** is a matter of taste. Haute Hijab treats it as advanced maximalism. Keep it off by default and allow it for a "bold" style profile.
- I found no source on Scandinavia specifically. The cold-weather rules (59) are the closest proxy.
