# Desi and Pakistani styling rules for Almari

How to read this: "src" refers to the numbered list at the end. Rules marked **[convention]** are common practice I could not tie to one authoritative editorial, so treat them as reasonable defaults rather than cited facts. A caveat on sources: most of what I found is brand blogs (Aza, Generation, Jazmin, Saira Shakira, HSY, Haute Hijab, Batik, Iznik/Vanya). They are practical but partly marketing. Vogue India, Diva and Something Haute did not come up with anything usable, and two pages (Lashkaraa, Haute Hijab) blocked direct fetch, so I used their search-indexed text.

## 1. Tops and bottoms

1. IF the top is a **gharara** THEN prefer a short or hip to mid-thigh kurti/kameez, and avoid knee-length or longer tops, because they hide the knee band (gota) that defines the gharara. (strong) - src 3
2. IF the top is a **sharara** THEN prefer a short kurti. A mid-length one is acceptable because the flare starts higher, and the top should still not reach the knee flare. (medium) - src 3
3. IF the top is an **anarkali** (flared, voluminous) THEN prefer churidar or slim fitted trousers. Avoid sharara, gharara and palazzo, since two volumes compete. (strong) - src 1
4. IF the top is a **long straight kameez** (knee length or longer) THEN prefer straight trousers, cigarette pants or shalwar, and avoid gharara. (strong) - src 1, 7
5. IF the top is a **short kurti** (hip to mid-thigh) THEN it works with jeans, cigarette pants, straight trousers, palazzo or sharara. A patiala/full shalwar pairs best with shorter kurtis. (medium) - src 1, 6
6. IF the bottom is **jeans** THEN prefer a short or medium kurti/tunic, and avoid a full-length kameez, a formal embroidered kameez or an anarkali. (medium) **[convention for the long-kameez part]** - src 6
7. IF a kurti is worn with **jeans** THEN prefer slim or straight, dark, clean denim (navy, black, grey). Avoid baggy, bootcut or ripped jeans. (medium) - src 6
8. IF the kurti is bright or printed THEN pair it with neutral bottoms. A pastel or white kurti can take lighter denim. (medium) - src 6
9. Never tuck a kurti or kameez. (strong) - src 6
10. IF the top is **heavily embellished or formal** THEN its bottom should be the matching formal one (silk or raw-silk trousers, sharara, gharara, churidar), not denim or casual cotton trousers. (strong) **[convention]**

## 2. Three-piece suits and mix-and-match

11. IF the suit is a **formal or embroidered 3-piece** (chiffon, organza, silk, velvet, worn to a wedding or Eid) THEN keep the set together, especially the dupatta, because it is designed as part of the embroidery. (strong) **[convention, consistent with src 8]**
12. IF the suit is **casual lawn or cotton** THEN mix-and-match is accepted. For example, wear the shirt with plain white trousers, the dupatta with another plain suit, or the trousers with a different kurti. (strong) - src 7, 8
13. IF the kameez is printed or heavily worked THEN prefer a solid or plain bottom and a solid, tone-on-tone dupatta. (strong) - src 8
14. IF the kameez is plain THEN a contrast or heavily embroidered dupatta is a favoured way to dress it up. (strong) - src 8
15. Prints: do not combine a printed kameez, a printed bottom and a different printed dupatta. Keep at most one printed statement piece, unless all three come from the same coordinated set. (medium) **[convention, supported by src 8 "bold print, keep the rest simple"]**
16. IF the print is subtle THEN statement jewellery is allowed. IF it is bold THEN keep accessories minimal. (medium) - src 8

## 3. Dupatta and hijab

17. IF the user wears hijab THEN default to a **separate hijab plus a dupatta draped on the shoulders or arms**. Dupattas used as hijab are common, but they are bulky and can be fiddly. (medium) - src 9, 10
18. IF the event is formal (barat, walima, nikah) and the user wears hijab THEN a valid option is a lightweight, pinned hijab with the dupatta laid over the head and shoulders on top of it. (medium) - src 9
19. IF the outfit is casual or work wear THEN skipping the dupatta is accepted in modern styling. Offer it as optional, not required. (medium, varies by family) - src 10
20. Hijab colour:
    - IF the kameez is printed THEN prefer a solid hijab in one of the print's colours.
    - IF the kameez is solid THEN a printed hijab is allowed.
    - A solid-on-solid or monochrome look is always safe. (strong) - src 11
21. IF a dupatta and a hijab are both worn THEN avoid three competing colours across hijab, dupatta and kameez. Match the hijab to either the kameez or the dupatta, or use a neutral. (medium) **[convention]**
22. Hijab fabric should follow the formality of the outfit: chiffon, georgette or silk-look hijabs for formal events, cotton or jersey for daily wear. (medium) **[convention]**
23. Jewellery metal should match the dupatta's thread work: gold work with gold jewellery, silver or multicolour with silver. (medium) - src 9, 12

## 4. Occasions and the formality ladder

24. Everyday and errands: printed lawn or cotton 2 or 3-piece, khaddar in winter, with straight or cigarette trousers. (strong) - src 7, 13
25. Work: clean cuts, soft prints, neutral or deep solids (navy, ivory). Avoid bling laces and shiny buttons. Straight shirt with cigarette or straight trousers. (strong) - src 13
26. Dawat or dinner: a lightly embroidered chiffon or "luxury pret" outfit. A printed lawn suit alone reads "too casual", while heavy formal reads "overdressed". (strong) - src 14
27. Mehndi or dholki: yellow, green, orange, pink, mint. Mirror work, gota, colourful thread. Sharara, gharara, lehenga or anarkali. (strong) - src 4, 15
28. Barat guest: the most formal level. Jewel or deep tones (maroon, emerald, royal blue, plum), zardozi, gota, heavy embroidery, velvet in winter, long flared silhouettes. (strong) - src 2, 4, 15
29. Walima guest: pastels (powder blue, blush, mint, lavender, ivory), delicate embroidery, structured or gown silhouettes. One step below barat in heaviness. (strong) - src 2, 15
30. Nikah guest: soft or ivory or pastel colours, modest, gharara is traditional. (medium) - src 3, 4
31. Eid: embroidered or handcrafted suit for the main day, printed pieces for daytime visits, khussa and jhumkas. (strong) - src 16
32. **"Dressed up enough" check:** IF the occasion is dawat or above THEN the outfit should contain at least one of: a formal fabric (chiffon, silk, organza, velvet, raw silk, net), visible embellishment, or a formal silhouette (sharara, gharara, anarkali, lehenga). Plain printed lawn fails this check. (medium) **[derived from src 13, 14]**

### Formality ladder

| Level | Fabrics | Embellishment | Silhouettes | Shoes and bag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Everyday / lawn casual | lawn, cotton, cambric, voile; khaddar or linen in winter | print, minimal thread | straight kameez, short kurti, straight or cigarette trousers, shalwar, jeans | flats, sandals, slides, casual khussa, kolhapuri; everyday bag |
| 2. Work | cotton, linen, lawn, georgette | none or minimal, no bling | straight kameez, cigarette or straight trousers, culottes | neat flats, low block heel |
| 3. Dawat / dinner / Eid visiting | chiffon, embroidered lawn, jacquard, karandi, khaddar (winter) | neckline or sleeve embroidery | straight or A-line shirt, trousers or shalwar, chiffon dupatta | embroidered khussa, block heels, sandals; small clutch |
| 4. Mehndi / dholki | chiffon, net, cotton silk, printed silk | mirror work, gota, colourful thread | sharara, gharara, lehenga, anarkali, angrakha | khussa (flat, for floor seating and dancing); potli |
| 5. Walima / nikah guest | chiffon, organza, silk, net, velvet (winter) | delicate embroidery, pearls, sequins | gown or maxi, anarkali, gharara, structured lehenga | heels or embellished khussa; clutch |
| 6. Barat guest | raw silk, velvet, organza, banarsi, net | heavy zardozi, dabka, gota, stones | lehenga, farshi or gharara, long flared kalidar | heels or heavily worked khussa; embellished clutch or potli |

## 5. Footwear and bags

33. Khussa works across levels 1 to 6. Match it by embellishment: a plain or printed khussa for casual wear, a gota or dabka khussa for barat, mirror or stone work for evening. (strong) - src 12, 17
34. IF the event involves long standing, floor seating or dancing (mehndi, long weddings) THEN prefer khussa or flats over heels. (medium) - src 12
35. Kolhapuri works with kurta plus trousers or chinos, and with casual or fusion outfits. Avoid it at barat or walima. (medium) - src 6 **[the "avoid at formal events" part is convention]**
36. White sneakers or loafers only go with fusion looks (kurti and jeans), never with formal Desi outfits. (medium) - src 6
37. Potli goes with festive and wedding Desi wear. A slim clutch suits an anarkali or formal outfit. IF the outfit is heavily embellished THEN use a minimal clutch. (medium) - src 18, 19
38. Do not pair formal heels or a potli with a casual lawn level-1 outfit. (weak) **[convention]**

## 6. Fusion with Western pieces

39. Short kurti with dark jeans is accepted for casual wear and university. (strong) - src 6
40. A denim jacket over a flowy kurti is accepted as casual fusion. (medium) - src 6
41. A blazer over a kameez or kurta (with jeans or straight trousers and boots) is accepted as smart or casual fusion. Avoid it over heavy formal wear. (medium) - src 20
42. A front-open long cardigan over shalwar kameez is a "timeless" accepted winter layer. (strong) - src 20, 21
43. A long coat or overcoat over a long shirt is accepted. Prefer coats that reach the kameez hem or longer, so the kameez does not stick out unevenly below a short jacket. (medium) - src 22 **[the hem-length part is convention]**
44. Clash: Western sportswear (hoodies, joggers, athletic sneakers) with formal or embroidered Desi pieces. (medium) **[convention]**
45. Clash: neon colours at any Desi event. They read as sporty or casual. (medium) - src 23

## 7. Colour conventions (these vary by community)

46. Mehndi: yellow and green are the anchor colours, with orange, pink and mint as companions. (strong) - src 4, 15
47. Barat: deep and jewel tones. Walima: pastels. (strong) - src 2, 15
48. Guests should avoid head-to-toe bridal red at barat. (strong, broadly shared) - src 23, 24
49. White: **contested.** In Hindu and many Indian traditions it means mourning and is avoided at weddings. Muslim and Pakistani weddings are described as more relaxed, and ivory or off-white is recommended for nikah and walima. Rule: IF wedding AND (user tradition = Hindu or Sikh, or family preference) THEN avoid white. Otherwise allow ivory or off-white at nikah or walima, but avoid stark all-white. Note that in Pakistan, plain white is also worn to funerals. (medium) - src 2, 15, 23
50. Black: **contested.** Avoided at Hindu and Sikh ceremonies, but listed as an appropriate barat colour in Pakistani sources, and increasingly accepted at evening receptions. Rule: allow black at Pakistani barat and evening events, and make it a user-configurable avoid. (medium) - src 2, 23
51. Funerals and condolence visits: plain, unembellished white or muted colours. (medium) **[convention, not sourced in this pass]**
52. Work palette: navy, ivory, pale pink, and earthy mustard, rust or cinnamon. (medium) - src 13

## 8. Winter and a Nordic climate

53. IF temperature is cold THEN prefer khaddar, karandi, marina, linen-wool, jacquard or velvet over lawn or chiffon. (strong) - src 22, 25
54. IF a karandi or khaddar suit is worn THEN layer it with a shawl, pashmina or wool dupatta. Neutral or contrasting shawls work, and the shawl may replace the dupatta. (strong) - src 22, 26
55. IF the occasion is casual winter daytime THEN muted karandi, a cosy shawl and khussa. IF it is a formal winter dinner or wedding THEN a darker karandi or velvet with rich embroidery and heels. (medium) - src 22
56. Karandi and khaddar suit straight or slightly A-line shirts with cigarette pants. (medium) - src 22
57. Velvet is the winter wedding fabric: deep tones for barat, pastel velvet for walima. (strong) - src 2
58. Nordic-specific, **[convention]**: outdoors, put a long wool coat over the outfit and swap the shawl in afterwards. Ankle boots are acceptable with trousers or cigarette pants on everyday days. Indoors at the event, keep the planned footwear (carry khussa or heels). Use warm leggings or thermals under the shalwar. A long fuzzy sweater with warm tights and high-tops is also cited. (medium) - src 21
59. Winter colour palette: deep blues, sage, berry, ice blue. (weak, one brand's view) - src 26

## Flags: contested or opinion-only

- White and black at weddings (rules 49, 50) differ sharply between Hindu/Indian and Pakistani/Muslim sources. Make them user or family settings.
- Skipping the dupatta (rule 19) depends on family and region. Some households expect one at any family gathering.
- How a hijabi combines dupatta and hijab (rules 17, 18, 21) is personal practice. There is no editorial standard.
- The "dressed up enough" threshold (rule 32) and the per-event colour lists come from brand blogs. Treat them as defaults, not law.
- Indian versus Pakistani terms: "kurti" in India means a short top. In Pakistan, "shirt" or "kameez" covers most lengths. Encode by length (hip, thigh, knee, calf), not by name.
