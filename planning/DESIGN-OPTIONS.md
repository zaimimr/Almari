# Visual comparison

Three original static iPhone concepts using an illustrative wardrobe. The user selected Direction A's background and overall look and feel. Native behavior, contrast, and accessibility still need implementation validation. Revised daily behavior is in [the Today brief](TODAY.md).

View all three in [the comparison gallery](index.html).

## A. The dressing room

[Open the mockup](concepts/a-dressing-room.png).

Warm flower white, taupe, and a muted plum action color. An editorial heading and a large garment collage make the daily outfit the focus. The first screen helps someone choose an occasion and create an outfit.

Strongest quality: inviting, personal, and immediately about wearing clothes already owned.

Risk: large display type and collage area reduce density. The final screen must remain usable at larger text sizes and on the smallest supported phone.

Carry forward: the visual hierarchy, real garment cutouts, quiet coverage summary, and explicit manual alternative. Simplify the introductory line and use consistent native icons in implementation.

Proposed palette: flower white `#FAF8F3`, warm sand `#EEE8DE`, bark `#322E28`, deep plum `#675469`, with a small sage accent.

## B. Olive atelier

[Open the mockup](concepts/b-olive-atelier.png).

Chalk, moss, and dark bark with a small mauve accent. System typography and an organized two-column closet let clothing carry the color and character. Search, filters, and Add a piece are immediately visible.

Strongest quality: a clear daily tool that scales to a larger wardrobe.

Risk: can resemble a retail catalog if labels, prices, and merchandising patterns creep in. Keep the language about personal ownership. The mockup's Style these pieces action needs explicit selection behavior in the real product.

Carry forward: the category navigation, calm density, natural garment images, and structured closet. Use one consistent symbol per tab; the concept images vary and are not the navigation specification.

Proposed palette: chalk `#F7F6F0`, moss `#56634B`, bark `#293026`, mauve `#A68DA8`.

## C. Rose journal

[Open the mockup](concepts/c-rose-journal.png).

Flower white, dusty rose, and plum. A saved outfit is presented like a page in a personal style journal. Make another like this is immediately associated with the saved look.

Strongest quality: expressive and well suited to revisiting favorite combinations.

Risk: the serif scale and nested visual containers can dominate smaller screens. A more restrained implementation should keep utility and accessibility clear. The exact garment shapes vary between concept images, so they must not be treated as a consistent wardrobe dataset.

Carry forward: saved-look naming, outfit collections, and a direct remix action. Collections are a proposed feature, not a confirmed first-release requirement.

Proposed palette: flower white `#FBF7F3`, warm ink `#4B3539`, rose `#A66F7A`, plum `#705167`.

## Selected direction

Use A as the visual foundation throughout the app. Its white/light neutral background keeps clothing and hijab colors prominent. The previous recommendation to blend directions is superseded by this user selection. Functional ideas from the study can be implemented within A's identity.

These original concepts show different product surfaces. Subsequent comparisons should refine specific layouts within the selected Direction A.

The outfit imagery illustrates Western layering, an abaya, a hijab, and a Pakistani kurta. It is not evidence of garment-recognition quality or a guarantee that an outfit matches a particular person's coverage requirements.

## What the next mockups should resolve

- Coverage setup with optional fit information and clear skip behavior.
- Photo review with original/cutout comparison and an uncertain-attribute state.
- A manual builder with multiple layers and matching-set support.
- A reference-image result that states which parts of the inspiration were preserved.
- A suggestion that cannot be completed from the owned wardrobe.
- A small closet, a large closet, long garment names, dark mode, and large text.

## Design exploration notes

Seven grounded directions considered: organized wardrobe archive, personal style journal, daily outfit planner, dressing-room table, stylist conversation, tailoring pattern book, and inspiration pinboard. The dressing-room structure leads this round. The archive and journal provide useful comparison surfaces within the requested palette.

The additional reference-setting, monochrome product marketing, cloud-construction, tension-network, emission-rail, and instrument-panel directions were declined on audience identification and product clarity after applying the actual closet tasks. Their specialized controls and diagram-like structures add translation work for daily dressing.

Useful disciplines retained for later implementation: explicit relationships between layers from reference-setting; explanations grounded in visible pieces from product marketing; predictable reversible edits from constructive interfaces; explicit layer dependencies from constraint networks; state descriptions that do not depend only on hue from the rail system; and stable positions for controls from instrument panels. These do not change the pinned earth and floral palette or native navigation.

The original B and C concepts remain as historical exploration. Current comparisons refine the chosen A identity and daily-outfit behavior. The project remains in the requested discussion and visual-mockup phase.
